# Using a reference video

Use this when recreating or adapting an existing video, or borrowing its structure for a new ad. The reference supplies evidence about the shots, story, and pacing. The user's brief determines which of those to retain.

## Inspect the source

Use `inspect_media` on the selected source and review the relevant returned contact sheets alongside its transcript and timing. Reuse a suitable existing inspection. Record the source link or file ID.

Notice the actual opening, the order and length of shots, the relationship between words and images, camera distance and movement, product handling, and the ending. Distinguish a picture cut from a change in narration: B-roll may cover a continuous spoken sentence.

Contact sheets contain sampled moments, not every frame. Treat estimated cut boundaries as estimates; use playback when the decision depends on motion, delivery, or a precise transition. Obtain individual source frames for image editing after selecting the shots. Frame extraction serves that purpose without requiring another content-analysis workflow.

## Decide what the reference controls

Make the intended relationship to the source explicit in the plan:

- **Same video, different person:** retain the shot sequence, product, actions, setting, framing, and expression. Apply the requested face, clothing, and color changes.
- **Adaptation for another product:** retain useful shot design and narrative roles, then change the script, product, branding, props, and actions as the new brief requires.
- **Structure or style inspiration:** borrow selected qualities such as the hook, pace, or kind of coverage. Create a new shot plan instead of implying a shot-for-shot recreation.

Follow the user's stated scope. A close recreation should preserve a phone-first opening if that is how the source starts; do not replace it with a conventional face-first hook simply because that is a common UGC pattern.

## Turn the breakdown into a new plan

Map each useful source beat to its role in the target video. A compact table can capture:

**Source time → picture/action → spoken cue → purpose → keep/change → target shot or frame.**

Preserve the source's meaningful relationships, not just its subject matter. A quick workout montage might supply activity evidence beneath a feature explanation; a held phone close-up might make that feature concrete. In a different category, cooking actions and a recipe screen could serve those same roles while retaining useful camera angles and physical staging.

Carry over pacing deliberately. If the reference uses brief activity cuts, do not expand each into the full length of a generated clip. If its demonstration needs time to read or understand, do not shorten it just to make every shot equally fast.

For a rewritten script, map the source's visual roles to the new spoken ideas. Use the new delivery to finalize cut timing rather than forcing its words into the original timestamps. Preserve exact timing when the user specifically requests it.

## Prepare the shot references

Follow [Creating realistic characters and shots](creating-a-realistic-character.md) for the image-edit workflow. Use each selected shot's actual individual frame as its composition reference, including B-roll and product shots. Establish the new character from a clear face frame, then reuse that identity reference across the other edits.

Retain source timestamps, original frames, selected edits, and prompts with the plan. Review original and edited frames side by side for the changes the brief requested and unintended drift.

## Continue into the requested deliverable

A reference-video workflow can end with a breakdown, plan, and still images. Generate footage only when it is part of the request.

Using a video as a creative reference does not automatically mean feeding that video into the generation model. A shot plan and adapted starting frames can guide separate A-roll and B-roll generations. Pass the original video directly only when the chosen generation workflow calls for it.

For the final sequence, return to [UGC video planning and structure](ugc-video-structure.md). Judge the result against both the target brief and the source qualities selected for preservation, and disclose meaningful departures.
