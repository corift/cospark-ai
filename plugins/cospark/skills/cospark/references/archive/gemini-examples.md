# Archived: Gemini Omni timed prompt examples

These examples accompany the [archived timed-action guide](gemini-talking-head.md). Do not load them for ordinary clip generation. Use the [active simple prompt guide](../gemini-talking-head.md) unless the user explicitly requests the former approach.

Adapt each example to the user's exact start frame, dialogue, voice, products, and requested action. Do not copy people, brands, props, or settings that are absent from the input.

## Product push toward the lens

This checkpoint pattern keeps most of the frame anchored while one product moves.

```text
A natural one-take smartphone UGC talking-head video. The camera stays fixed. Preserve the woman's identity, apartment setting, outfit, and exactly three David Protein Ice Cream tubs from the starting frame. Keep her hands and product packaging coherent and realistic.

She speaks directly to the camera in a casual, excited young female UGC voice, with warm eye contact and energetic but believable delivery.

Dialogue [0-6s]: "These David protein ice creams are insane, and they're my go-to."
Action [0-2s]: She begins with a small knowing smile and slight eyebrow lift, keeping both product groups visible.
Action [2-4.5s]: On the word "insane," she smoothly moves the single tub in her left hand toward the camera until it becomes the foreground focus, without covering her whole face.
Action [4.5-6.5s]: As she says "they're my go-to," she smoothly pulls that tub back to its original chest-level position and gives one small affirmative nod.
Action [6.5-8s]: She holds the products naturally, maintains eye contact, and finishes with a relaxed closed-mouth smile.

No cuts, no camera movement, no added people, no extra products, no captions, no background music, and no additional spoken words.
```

Why it works:

- The starting frame already contains the person and products.
- Exactly one tub moves; the stacked pair remains a visual anchor.
- The product push is attached to the emphasis word “insane.”
- The pullback has enough time to finish before the clip ends.

## Fast explanatory “yapper” gesture

Use when both hands are free and the dialogue contains a question followed by a reveal.

```text
A natural one-take smartphone UGC talking-head video. The camera stays fixed. Preserve the woman, room, outfit, lighting, and framing from the starting image. Keep both hands anatomically coherent and inside the portrait frame.

She speaks directly to the camera in a quick, conversational young female UGC voice, as though explaining something surprising to a close friend.

Dialogue [0-7s]: "Okay, but where are AI UGC creators actually finding paid gigs? Because it isn't where you think."
Action [0-2s]: On "Okay," she briefly brings her fingertips together, then opens both palms in a compact questioning gesture.
Action [2-5s]: On "actually," she leans forward slightly and makes one small outward hand circle while maintaining eye contact.
Action [5-7s]: On "isn't where you think," her hands settle lower and she gives a small knowing head tilt.
Action [7-8s]: She holds eye contact and finishes with a restrained half-smile.

No cuts, no camera movement, no captions, no music, and no additional spoken words.
```

## High-gesture direct-response hook

Use when the user explicitly requests noticeably more hand gestures, the start frame leaves both hands visible, and the dialogue contains multiple ideas that can support sequential beats. This mode uses more motion than the default without asking for uncontrolled continuous gesturing.

```text
A natural one-take smartphone UGC talking-head video. The camera stays fixed. Preserve the man's identity, outdoor setting, outfit, lighting, and portrait framing from the starting image. Keep both forearms and hands visible inside the portrait frame, anatomically coherent, and moving in active but believable conversational “yapper” gestures throughout.

He speaks directly to the camera in a quick but clear, credible male UGC voice, with natural eye contact, restrained energy, and noticeably more hand expression.

Dialogue [0-10s]: "[Exact direct-response hook.]"
Action [0-2.5s]: On "[audience qualifier]," he raises both hands to chest level, briefly brings loose fingertips toward each other, then opens both palms outward while leaning in slightly.
Action [2.5-5.5s]: On "[first side of contrast]," he gestures once to his left with an open hand; on "[second side of contrast]," he contrasts it with one open-hand gesture to his right.
Action [5.5-7.5s]: On "[invitation or promise]," he brings one hand forward in a compact palm-up invitation while the other remains naturally active at chest level.
Action [7.5-10s]: On "[explanation phrase]," he makes one small outward hand circle; on "[concluding phrase]," both hands settle into an open, confident gesture and he finishes with a small nod.

No cuts, no camera movement, no added people or objects, no broad smile, no frantic or oversized arm motions, no captions, no background music, and no additional spoken words.
```

Why it works:

- “More gestures” becomes a sequence of concrete, non-overlapping actions.
- Each gesture has a semantic reason and is anchored to spoken language.
- Both hands stay within a protected, anatomically plausible working area.
- The final phrase includes its own settling motion, so dense dialogue can use the full clip duration.

## Left-and-right contrast

Use for before/after, misconception/reality, or option A/option B dialogue.

```text
A natural one-take smartphone UGC talking-head video. The camera remains fixed. Preserve the man, clothing, room, lighting, and framing from the starting image. His forearms and hands remain visible and realistic.

He speaks directly to the camera in a confident but friendly male UGC voice.

Dialogue [0-6.5s]: "Most people chase more views. The creators getting paid are making better examples."
Action [0-2.5s]: On "more views," he gestures once toward his left with an open hand.
Action [2.5-5.5s]: On "getting paid," he gestures once toward his right, clearly contrasting the second idea with the first.
Action [5.5-8s]: He brings both hands back to a relaxed position, gives one small nod, and maintains direct eye contact.

No cuts, no zoom, no camera movement, no captions, no music, and no additional spoken words.
```

## Late object pickup

Use only when the object is already visible and within believable reach. A late pickup is usually more coherent than manipulating the object continuously while speaking.

```text
A natural one-take smartphone UGC talking-head video. The camera stays fixed. Preserve the woman, bathroom vanity, outfit, skincare jar, lighting, and framing from the starting image. The jar remains on the counter until she picks it up with her right hand.

She speaks directly to the camera in a warm, trustworthy female UGC voice.

Dialogue [0-6s]: "The expensive routine wasn't what fixed my skin. Consistency with this was."
Action [0-3s]: She makes one compact palm-up gesture with her left hand while her right hand stays relaxed near the jar.
Action [3-5.5s]: On "this," she reaches with her right hand, picks up the existing jar once, and raises it to chest height with the label facing the camera.
Action [5.5-8s]: She keeps the jar in the same right hand, gives a small affirmative nod, and holds it steadily beside her face.

The jar never switches hands, disappears, duplicates, or changes shape. No cuts, no camera movement, no added products, no captions, no music, and no additional spoken words.
```
